## Solver-free stop signal

Stop training when, on the net's own first-player tree, every raw move agrees with the net's own search (zero disagreements).

**Conclusion.** Safe and timely on tic-tac-toe with siblings (h69) and without (h74): 0 false stops, latency 0-1 iterations. On Connect-4 it never fires (h88). Most disagreements there are harmless — both moves optimal (59%) — or the search's fault (16%) (h90), but none of the nets is certified through 10 plies (h91) and at the first failing ply the net and its 200-sim search mostly agree on the losing move (h92): an agreement signal cannot be made safe on Connect-4 until the opening labels are right. Counting equal-value moves as agreement does not rescue it. A value-aware reading (a move also agrees within search Q 0.1 of the top move) passed the tic-tac-toe recalibration (h101), but only vacuously: it never differed from the share reading there (h102). On the six T10 Connect-4 nets it also equals the share reading at delta 0.05 and 0.1, and delta 0.2 removes at most 21% (h103, h105): the search itself values the rejected moves more than 0.1 lower, so the share rule already is a value rule. The value head is part of the cause but not all of it: it backs 42% of the nets' wrong moves (h106, inconclusive between the 40% and 60% bars), 57% at plies 0-4, and at the empty board every value head rates the winning centre below at least two other moves (h107). The rest are policy-side, where the value head ranks the right move higher one move ahead — yet the net's own 200-sim search labels an optimal move there only 47% of the time (h108, inconclusive), 15% at plies 2-4, and only 18% where the value head backs the error (h109). In the opening the training target itself is wrong at most of the nets' errors, so the value signal the search reads, not how the policy fits its labels, is the lever.

## Relabelling with a deeper search (reanalyse)

Relabel the whole buffer each iteration with a 200-sim search of the current net.

**Conclusion.** Works and replicates on tic-tac-toe, through the policy prior (h25, h26, h34, h35, h36, h37), but is a partial cause of the fix, not the whole of it (h28). On Connect-4 a 200-sim relabel search is silently wrong in the opening (h92), and more search does not fix it: 200 / 2,000 / 20,000 sims are right at 86.5 / 87.2 / 83.7% of opening positions and at only 33-60% of the first moves, 56% at 100,000 (h96; h93 inconclusive by its bars). The search reads the value head, so the value signal is the lever (stop_signal, h106-h109): value targets for the strategy-tree positions, from the relabel search's root value, do no harm on tic-tac-toe (h110: 10/10 seeds still perfect from the start, stop still safe). On Connect-4 neither they nor n-step bootstrapped value targets help: in a paired 4-seed pilot the share of optimal moves at the nets' own opening positions fell from 86% to 82% and 75% (h111, h112, both refuted). Both targets come from the net itself, and the readout shows the echo: with search-value targets the value head backs 73% of the opening errors, against 36% without (h113). A value target the net computes cannot add information it lacks; it has to come from real game outcomes. The backward curriculum (games started near the end, moving back) is the first such fix; it does no harm on tic-tac-toe (h114: 10/10 seeds still perfect from the start, stop safe), but in a paired 4-seed Connect-4 pilot it did not raise the share of optimal opening moves (72% vs 73%, 1 win of 4; h115 refuted). That pilot measure is noisy: the unchanged process scores 86% and 73% on two sets of four seeds, and single nets range from 62% to 94% (h116), so the three refutations rule out large gains only. The next pilot must be powered first. Scored on one fixed set of 5,142 exactly valued positions instead, nets of one arm spread 3.5 points and seed-matched differences 3.1, so 7 seed pairs detect a 3-point gain and 16 a 2-point one (h117); post hoc on that set all three refuted treatments read 1.7-2.1 points above base, a hypothesis that the powered pre-registered run (four arms, seven fresh seeds, Holm-corrected permutation tests) mostly did not reproduce: tree values -0.4 and the curriculum -2.3 points (h119, h120 refuted); only n-step kept its direction, +3.4 points on 5 of 7 pairs, uncorrected p 0.031, short of Holm's bar (h118 inconclusive; h121). No net in any arm is certified through 6 plies. Next real-outcome fix: an exploiter in self-play (the current net with 8x search, greedy, in half the games); its first tic-tac-toe no-harm run could not be judged because the run recorder missed the exploiter games (h122 not run; dead end h123, fixed); rerun on fresh seeds it does no harm (h124: 10/10 seeds still perfect from the start, stop safe); but on Connect-4 it changed nothing: +0.06 points on the fixed set (p 0.48; h125 inconclusive) and 71.1% vs 71.2% optimal at plies 0-4 (h126), though it played ~470 games per seed. Of the five fixes tried, only n-step shows a lead, and none makes the opening right (0 certified nets in every arm). Running the base process three times as long settles why: from iteration 20 to 60 general play keeps improving (+5.5 points on the fixed set, h128) but the opening does not move (+0.5 points at plies 0-4, h127 refuted; one seed fell from 75% to 68%). The opening is not waiting on a longer run — this self-play process does not learn it at this scale, whatever the value-signal fix. The hybrid's first measurement: an exact opening exception table computed by the process, with the net carrying the next ply, certifies on none of the ten nets at any horizon (h129, h130 refuted). The table overrides 50%, 26% and 15% of its positions before plies 3, 5 and 7, and the net is wrong at 23%, 12% and 12% of the ply it carries (h131): it improves with depth but its tree grows ~6x per ply. A hybrid certified through 10 would need ~190 table entries per net (~12% of its first-player positions). Training the net beside the table works: with self-play started at the table's frontier and the strategy tree walked from there, the net plays the optimal move at 81.6% of the 284 positions one ply past the table against 75.0% for the base process (+6.5 points, 6 of 7 seed pairs, p 0.016; h132 supported) and fails less at the never-trained ply 8 too (681 vs 865; h133). No hybrid certifies through 9 plies yet. Training the net on both plies it carries (6 and 8) from the frontier (H3) could not be judged: it was launched with 6 relabel workers against the registered 4 (h138; h134, h135 inconclusive by protocol). With the run's own worker count the registered tests are still inconclusive — +1.2 points at ply 8 (5 of 7, p 0.14) and a ply-6 curve that has flattened (+0.6 points over the last ten passes, most of the gain by pass 10; h139). H3 needs fewer table exceptions on 6 of 7 seeds (253 vs 278 entries; h141), but every certified-through-9 hybrid is over 25x the size of a table alone over the same tree: the 20,616-parameter net at 32 bits dominates, and break-even is about 1 bit per weight (h140). Through ply 9 the smallest certified description is the table. Priced properly — a complete table is one 3-bit move per first-player position in walk order, exceptions the cheaper of an index or a mask (h140 superseded) — the break-even precision roughly quadruples every two plies: ~0.2 bits per weight at depth 9, ~1 at 11, 3.7-4.35 at 13 (h144; the registered bar of 4 at depth 13 split, h143 inconclusive). Where and why the nets fail: localisation, replication, starvation, the metric itself.

**Conclusion.** The blind spot is systematic and replicates (h11, h18, h24), is not explained by the eval budget (h12) or off-manifold enrichment (h14, h16), and self-play never trains the positions involved (h30). The coverage metric was wrong until it read every orientation (h39). Connect-4 accuracy away from training data is data-limited (h48).

## Measurement integrity

Resume, eras and fingerprints, the register, the statistics, mutation testing: the guards that keep results honest.

**Conclusion.** Each guard exists because an earlier reading was wrong without it, and each is a supported, test-backed claim; the two latest dead ends were a register hook that applied one arm's outcome to every arm (h82) and a hand-run mutation hidden by stale bytecode (h83). A pool deadlock voided 2.3 h of solving: one ProcessPoolExecutor.map over ~190,000 jobs blocked on Python 3.10's executor wake-up pipe (CPython gh-105829); exact solves now go in slices of at most 2,048 (h151). The same batch had passed once before — a race that passes is not evidence of safety.

rests on: h82 (supported), h83 (supported), t11 (supported), t15 (supported), t27 (supported), h151 (supported)

- **t1** SUPPORTED — A resumed run keeps its optimizer state, so an interruption does not change the experiment
- **t2** SUPPORTED — An orphan checkpoint is retrained rather than stranding the run's ledger-readability
- **t3** SUPPORTED — A killed buffer write leaves the previous buffer intact
- **t4** SUPPORTED — A run whose training code moved underneath it is refused resumption

- **t6** SUPPORTED — A compute-matched pair whose game counts differ by design is accepted, not refused
- **t9** SUPPORTED — A sleep assertion alone does not protect a long run: on battery caffeinate -s is inert, so the guard must check power source too
- **t10** SUPPORTED — An arm name means ONE measurement on ONE root family — reusing it across families is refused, and refused WITHOUT destroying the original outcomes
