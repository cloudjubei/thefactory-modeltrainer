## Solver-free stop signal

Stop training when, on the net's own first-player tree, every raw move agrees with the net's own search (zero disagreements).

**Conclusion.** Safe and timely on tic-tac-toe with siblings (h69) and without (h74): 0 false stops, latency 0-1 iterations. On Connect-4 it never fires (h88). Most disagreements there are harmless — both moves optimal (59%) — or the search's fault (16%) (h90), but none of the nets is certified through 10 plies (h91) and at the first failing ply the net and its 200-sim search mostly agree on the losing move (h92): an agreement signal cannot be made safe on Connect-4 until the opening labels are right. Counting equal-value moves as agreement does not rescue it. A value-aware reading (a move also agrees within search Q 0.1 of the top move) passed the tic-tac-toe recalibration (h101), but only vacuously: it never differed from the share reading there (h102). On the six T10 Connect-4 nets it also equals the share reading at delta 0.05 and 0.1, and delta 0.2 removes at most 21% (h103, h105): the search itself values the rejected moves more than 0.1 lower, so the share rule already is a value rule. The value head is part of the cause but not all of it: it backs 42% of the nets' wrong moves (h106, inconclusive between the 40% and 60% bars), 57% at plies 0-4, and at the empty board every value head rates the winning centre below at least two other moves (h107). The rest are policy-side, where the value head ranks the right move higher one move ahead — yet the net's own 200-sim search labels an optimal move there only 47% of the time (h108, inconclusive), 15% at plies 2-4, and only 18% where the value head backs the error (h109). In the opening the training target itself is wrong at most of the nets' errors, so the value signal the search reads, not how the policy fits its labels, is the lever.

## Relabelling with a deeper search (reanalyse)

Relabel the whole buffer each iteration with a 200-sim search of the current net.

**Conclusion.** Works and replicates on tic-tac-toe, through the policy prior (h25, h26, h34, h35, h36, h37), but is a partial cause of the fix, not the whole of it (h28). On Connect-4 a 200-sim relabel search is silently wrong in the opening (h92), and more search does not fix it: 200 / 2,000 / 20,000 sims are right at 86.5 / 87.2 / 83.7% of opening positions and at only 33-60% of the first moves, 56% at 100,000 (h96; h93 inconclusive by its bars). The search reads the value head, so the value signal is the lever (stop_signal, h106-h109): value targets for the strategy-tree positions, from the relabel search's root value, do no harm on tic-tac-toe (h110: 10/10 seeds still perfect from the start, stop still safe). On Connect-4 neither they nor n-step bootstrapped value targets help: in a paired 4-seed pilot the share of optimal moves at the nets' own opening positions fell from 86% to 82% and 75% (h111, h112, both refuted). Both targets come from the net itself, and the readout shows the echo: with search-value targets the value head backs 73% of the opening errors, against 36% without (h113). A value target the net computes cannot add information it lacks; it has to come from real game outcomes. The backward curriculum (games started near the end, moving back) is the first such fix; it does no harm on tic-tac-toe (h114: 10/10 seeds still perfect from the start, stop safe), but in a paired 4-seed Connect-4 pilot it did not raise the share of optimal opening moves (72% vs 73%, 1 win of 4; h115 refuted). That pilot measure is noisy: the unchanged process scores 86% and 73% on two sets of four seeds, and single nets range from 62% to 94% (h116), so the three refutations rule out large gains only. The next pilot must be powered first. Scored on one fixed set of 5,142 exactly valued positions instead, nets of one arm spread 3.5 points and seed-matched differences 3.1, so 7 seed pairs detect a 3-point gain and 16 a 2-point one (h117); post hoc on that set all three refuted treatments read 1.7-2.1 points above base, a hypothesis that the powered pre-registered run (four arms, seven fresh seeds, Holm-corrected permutation tests) mostly did not reproduce: tree values -0.4 and the curriculum -2.3 points (h119, h120 refuted); only n-step kept its direction, +3.4 points on 5 of 7 pairs, uncorrected p 0.031, short of Holm's bar (h118 inconclusive; h121). No net in any arm is certified through 6 plies. Next real-outcome fix: an exploiter in self-play (the current net with 8x search, greedy, in half the games); its tic-tac-toe no-harm check is h122 (pending).

## Diagnosing failures (the tic-tac-toe blind spot)

Where and why the nets fail: localisation, replication, starvation, the metric itself.

**Conclusion.** The blind spot is systematic and replicates (h11, h18, h24), is not explained by the eval budget (h12) or off-manifold enrichment (h14, h16), and self-play never trains the positions involved (h30). The coverage metric was wrong until it read every orientation (h39). Connect-4 accuracy away from training data is data-limited (h48).

rests on: h11 (supported), h12 (refuted), h14 (refuted), h16 (supported), h18 (supported), h24 (supported), h30 (supported), h39 (supported), h48 (supported)

- **h11** SUPPORTED — The tic-tac-toe coverage blind spot is SYSTEMATIC: independently trained seeds fail at the SAME states beyond a uniform random-subset null over the failable universe (p < 0.0125). False => the ceiling is seed noise.
- **h12** REFUTED — The blind spot lives in the NET, not the deployment budget: 8x more eval search (48->400 sims, fresh tree) fixes fewer than half the failures (one-sided Wilson upper bound < 0.5 at alpha 0.0125).
- **h13** SUPPORTED — Failing states were STARVED of training data: fewer final-replay-buffer visits than non-failing failable states of the same seed and ply (stratified permutation, p < 0.0125).
- **h14** REFUTED — Failing states are ENRICHED off the optimal-play manifold (reachable only after a blunder) beyond the base rate (exact hypergeometric over distinct failable states, p < 0.0125).
- **h15** SUPPORTED — On tic-tac-toe the deep-search check CANNOT attribute the blind spot to net vs budget: an untrained net at 400 sims fixes 72% of the failing states (Wilson lower bound > 0.5) — search alone nearly exhausts the tree. h12's refutation is real but uninformative.
- **h16** SUPPORTED — Failing states are NOT enriched off the optimal-play manifold: 13/17 off vs an 80% base rate (hypergeometric p=0.76) — the blind spot is not 'positions only reached after a blunder'.
- **h17** SUPPORTED — Tic-tac-toe coverage is mostly SEARCH: an untrained net with 48-sim search already covers 0.935; training lifts it to 0.982-0.994 and lifts the raw policy from 0.54 to 0.94-0.96. A tic-tac-toe coverage number must be read against this search-alone control.
- **h18** SUPPORTED — REPLICATION: fresh baseline seeds (11-20) misplay the 17 localized target states far beyond the target's share of the failable universe (hypergeometric p < 0.0125) — the §C.43 blind spot is real out of sample.
- **h24** SUPPORTED — §C.45 G1: the tic-tac-toe blind spot REPLICATES a third time — base seeds 21-40 misplay the 17 target states beyond their universe share (hypergeometric p<0.05).
- **h30** SUPPORTED — §C.45: 200-sim self-play (after 2 random plies) never trains on off-manifold positions where O (to move) wins — zero such visits in 20/20 mixed_deep seeds vs visited in >=15/20 mixed seeds — and its decline sits on O-to-move target states (paired sign-flip p<1e-3). Mechanism (value head learns 'O to move in an odd position loses') is a hypothesis, not shown.
- **h39** SUPPORTED — §C.46 CORRECTION OF THE METRIC: every coverage number since §C.41 scored the raw policy on ONE canonical image per state. The net is not exactly symmetric: rebuilt bit-for-bit, the 20 R200S nets are perfect on 9/20 seeds canonically but on 0/20 over every reachable raw position (4,520), and on 16/20 when the policy is averaged over the 8 verified images (an evaluation-time ensemble, a different policy). RxS: 12/20, 0/20, 17/20. The floor test's operator was never pre-registered and decides its verdict.
- **h41** SUPPORTED — §C.47 F1: the generic process (R200S recipe unchanged, seeds 81-100) meets the tic-tac-toe floor under the symmetry-averaged policy — at least 15 of 20 seeds perfect at every position (refuted at <= 11, inconclusive 12-14, not_run on any integrity failure)
- **h42** SUPPORTED — §C.47 F2: on the same seeds the symmetry-averaged policy is perfect on more seeds than the strict raw reading (exact one-sided sign test on discordant seeds, p < 0.01; refuted if the reverse reaches it)
- **h48** SUPPORTED — §C.48 T3 (descriptive): Connect-4 accuracy on unseen non-trivial positions (plies 14-38) is DATA-limited — every net improves with every tripling of exact labels (4k -> 12k -> 35k); the 1.8M net reaches ~0.82 and fits its training set, the 20K/60K nets cannot fit theirs
- **t25** SUPPORTED — Optimality is measurable as STATE-SPACE COVERAGE: the symmetry-reduced fraction of reachable states where an agent's move is in the solver's optimal set — an optimal agent scores 1.0, a weaker one strictly less, and a per-ply breakdown localizes where it fails
- **t26** SUPPORTED — Coverage is a property of a POLICY: an act_fn that keeps state between calls (a search agent reusing its tree across states) is REFUSED, because its score depends on evaluation order — measured 0.9904 forward vs 0.9968 reversed on one tictactoe net; per_state_act (fresh agent + rng per state) is order-invariant (0.9872 both ways).

## Measurement integrity

Resume, eras and fingerprints, the register, the statistics, mutation testing: the guards that keep results honest.

**Conclusion.** Each guard exists because an earlier reading was wrong without it, and each is a supported, test-backed claim; the two latest dead ends were a register hook that applied one arm's outcome to every arm (h82) and a hand-run mutation hidden by stale bytecode (h83).

rests on: h82 (supported), h83 (supported), t11 (supported), t15 (supported), t27 (supported)

- **t1** SUPPORTED — A resumed run keeps its optimizer state, so an interruption does not change the experiment
- **t2** SUPPORTED — An orphan checkpoint is retrained rather than stranding the run's ledger-readability
- **t3** SUPPORTED — A killed buffer write leaves the previous buffer intact
- **t4** SUPPORTED — A run whose training code moved underneath it is refused resumption
- **t5** SUPPORTED — 'final' is earned only by reaching the declared batch count, never by being newest
- **t6** SUPPORTED — A compute-matched pair whose game counts differ by design is accepted, not refused
- **t9** SUPPORTED — A sleep assertion alone does not protect a long run: on battery caffeinate -s is inert, so the guard must check power source too
- **t10** SUPPORTED — An arm name means ONE measurement on ONE root family — reusing it across families is refused, and refused WITHOUT destroying the original outcomes
- **t11** SUPPORTED — A stale bytecode cache can invert a mutation verdict, so a mutation run must clear it — the guard checks the poisoned IMPORT, not that caches were deleted
- **t12** SUPPORTED — A guard that still runs but AFTER the damage is a distinct, detectable mutation — relocation needs multi-edit support, which single-substitution mutation testing cannot express
- **t13** SUPPORTED — A superseded claim keeps its own derived status and gains a pointer — retiring a finding must not misreport what its evidence actually found
- **t14** SUPPORTED — A comparison drawn at one arm's own TRAINING search budget is flagged as home ground; arms measured at different deployment budgets are refused as unpaired
- **t15** SUPPORTED — A claim must clear the null band ITS OWN registrant declared — a significant effect smaller than that band reads NEGLIGIBLE, not supported
- **t16** SUPPORTED — A run's cost is summed over EVERY segment and quoted as CPU time; a run whose CPU/wall ratio falls below its thread count is reported as sleep-contaminated, never returned as a clean number
- **t17** SUPPORTED — A stored comparison keeps the CAVEATS it was drawn with, and they travel into the register's evidence — a verdict that outlives the reasons to doubt it is prose with a p-value
- **t18** SUPPORTED — One comparison read at several settings is summarised by a POOLED effect with the interaction tested and handed back — reading each setting alone invites the significant/non-significant fallacy
- **t21** SUPPORTED — A change's effect across games is read as ONE verdict with multiplicity counted across the row (N games = N comparisons at alpha/N): generalises only if every game moves the same way, contested if signs disagree, local if only some move, null vs inconclusive split by the null band
- **t22** SUPPORTED — generalises (a conjunction: every game moved) is an intersection-union test at alpha, NOT Bonferroni alpha/N — the corrected bar is for the union question (did any game move); and null requires the whole CI inside the null band, not a small point estimate
- **t23** SUPPORTED — A cross-game matrix cell's unit of replication is the TRAINING RUN, not the opening: a single training draw is never significant and carries a standing caveat; significance comes from a between-seed t-test over per-seed deltas
- **t27** SUPPORTED — A test-backed claim about STORED evidence is timed against the data's own 'started', not the verify call — else a claim written after reading the results reads as a prediction (h15-h17 did, until fixed). Declared data cannot be swapped or regenerated under a claim.
- **t28** SUPPORTED — A failed test-backed claim is not automatically REFUTED: when its declared inconclusive_proof passes (a gate failed, or the data only bound the effect) it reads INCONCLUSIVE. Without this the register turned §C.44's underpowered nulls into refutations.
- **t29** SUPPORTED — A sign-flip confidence bound sits ON a subset mean of the deltas, often exactly a pre-registered threshold (0.5 = 3/6); threshold comparisons read an exact tie as inside the closed confidence set. Found by the §C.45 implementation review: bisection overshot the tie by ~5e-14 and would have turned a clean fix into 'moved' in ~4-11% of runs.
- **t30** SUPPORTED — A test-backed claim must DECLARE what its proof reads — a data file (even one not yet produced, timed when verify first finds it) or reads-no-data — so a data-backed claim can never be timed by its own verify call. §C.45 nearly verified h24-h27 without their data attached; the rule made remembering unnecessary.
- **t31** SUPPORTED — A net arch that sets residual-only settings (blocks, head_hidden, batchnorm, global_pool) without residual=True is REFUSED, in arch_for_game and in validate_config. §C.43-§C.45 recorded {32,3,32} and silently trained a 12,746-parameter legacy net; the G0 gate then showed that net cannot fit the exact policy at the recipe's budget.
- **t32** SUPPORTED — A sibling treatment counts as delivered only if it CHANGED training: the treatment's first relabelled pass must end with different weights from the control's, since everything before it is byte-identical by construction. Found by the §C.46 implementation review (the delivery gate read what train_net was handed, not what it trained on).
- **t33** SUPPORTED — A claim reading several evidence files is timed against the EARLIEST of them, all must exist before verify, and none may be regenerated — the §C.46 critic found h33-h37 timed against one arm (R200S) while they judge others.
- **h82** SUPPORTED — §C.48 DEAD END (guarded): the register's test hook matched PARAMETRIZED proofs by function name, so one arm's recorded outcome was applied to every arm. Fixed: proofs are matched by the full parametrized test name.
- **h83** SUPPORTED — §C.32/§C.49 DEAD END (guarded): a hand-run mutation of the same byte length, restored within the same second, left the mutant's bytecode valid, so tests ran the mutant after 'restoring' (relapsed 2026-09-30). Fixed rule: mutations run only through scripts/mutate.py, which never writes bytecode.
